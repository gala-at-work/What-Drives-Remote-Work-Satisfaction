# What-Drives-Remote-Work-Satisfaction

The project aims to identify the workplace and lifestyle factors most associated with employee stress and remote-work satisfaction, and translate the findings into actionable recommendations for HR and people-operations teams.

An exploratory data analysis of how remote, hybrid, and onsite work arrangements relate to employee mental health, stress, sleep, productivity, and job satisfaction. Built on a dataset of ~5,000 employees spanning multiple industries, job roles, and global regions.

Identify the workplace and lifestyle factors most associated with employee stress and remote-work satisfaction, and translate the findings into actionable

recommendations for HR and people-operations teams.

### This projects helps answer business questions like:

- Should comapanies subsidize gym memberships or activity programs?; Does physical activity actually reduce stress and lift productivity enough to justify the investment?

- Are we getting a return on our mental health resource spend?; Do employees with access to mental health resources actually report lower stress? If not, the budget may need to shift from access to adoption or quality.

- Should we do soemthing to keep remote workers connected?; Is social isolation dragging down remote-work satisfaction
